# What the chat experience actually looks like

The point of [agent-led sales](./concepts.md) is that there are **two
conversations** happening at once:

1. **You ↔ your agent** — feels like texting a capable friend.
2. **Your agent ↔ the business** — where the actual sale happens, in language, not
   on a webpage. *(This is "conversation as conversion" — points.md P4.)*

You only ever see the first one. The second is where a business wins or loses.

---

## Example: the shoes

### What you see

> **You:** macy's got a meet saturday and her trail shoes have a hole in them 🙄 can you sort it
>
> **Agent:** On it. Two quick things — roughly what size is she now, and any budget I should stay under?
>
> **You:** like a kids 5? not sure honestly. nothing crazy, under 100
>
> **Agent:** Got it. Saturday meet means it realistically needs to ship tomorrow, so I'm only looking at stuff that'll actually arrive in time. Give me a sec.
>
> **Agent:** Three that work:
> 1. **Saucony Peregrine, $74** — arrives Thursday, reviews say true to size 👍
> 2. **Merrell trail, $58** — cheaper, but a lot of "runs small, size up" reviews, so size 5 is risky
> 3. **Brooks, $92** — great shoe but won't land till Monday ❌ too late
>
> I'd go #1 — in budget, here in time, no sizing drama. Want me to grab them?
>
> **You:** yeah do it
>
> **Agent:** Done — $74, Saucony Peregrine size 5, arriving Thursday. I used your usual card, confirmation's in your email. 🎉
>
> **You:** 🙏

That's the whole experience. Twenty seconds of thumbs. You never saw a website.

### What happened behind the glass

While you waited that "give me a sec," your agent was having a *different*
conversation — with the stores. Roughly:

> **Agent → Store A:** Kids trail running shoe, size 5, under $100, needs to arrive by Friday. What've you got?
>
> **Store A:** Saucony Peregrine kids, size 5 in stock, $74. Standard shipping arrives Thursday. Free returns. Reviews skew true-to-size.
>
> *(clean answer → makes the shortlist)*
>
> **Agent → Store B:** Same question.
>
> **Store B:** *(no straight answer — can't confirm size 5 stock or a delivery date without a human checking out)*
>
> *(no answer → quietly dropped, never mentioned to you)*

Store A didn't win because it had a nicer site or a coupon. It won because when the
agent *asked*, it could answer — fast, specific, and in a way the agent could act on.
Store B might've had the better shoe. It just couldn't hold up its side of the
conversation, so it was never in the running.

## Example: the SaaS capability

Here the "you" is a builder, and the chat is with your coding agent while it works.

### What you see

> **You:** build the part that turns uploaded episodes into searchable transcripts
>
> **Agent:** That needs a transcription service — we don't have one wired up. I can pull one in. Fine if it's pay-per-use and costs a few cents to test?
>
> **You:** yeah keep it cheap
>
> **Agent:** Found one — pennies per minute, no signup wall, I can just call it. Running a 30-second test now…
>
> **Agent:** Works — transcript came back clean, that test cost $0.004. Wiring it into the upload flow. Anything I touch here you'll see in the diff.
>
> **You:** 👍

You never picked a vendor. You set a constraint ("keep it cheap") and approved a
direction. The agent did the shopping inside the task.

### What happened behind the glass

> **Agent → Service A:** Need speech-to-text. What's the price, and can I call it right now without creating an account or a trial?
>
> **Service A:** $0.006/audio-minute, pay-as-you-go. Here's the endpoint and a key flow you can do programmatically — no human step. Go ahead.
>
> *(adoptable mid-task → gets used)*
>
> **Agent → Service B:** Same question.
>
> **Service B:** Start your 14-day free trial, then verify your email, then contact sales for API access.
>
> *(every step is a gate → agent backs out)*

Service B is probably more accurate. It didn't matter. The agent wasn't making a
big considered purchase — it had a ten-minute itch. The service that let itself be
*used right now, for almost nothing* won. The one built to be *sold* lost, because
nobody was in a buying-process mood — they were just trying to get unstuck.

## Example: the freelance offer

The "you" is the customer again; the chat is with your personal agent.

### What you see

> **You:** my resume's three years stale and a job I want closes thursday. find someone good to fix it, fast
>
> **Agent:** Want a full rewrite or just a cleanup? And should they tailor it to that specific job posting?
>
> **You:** full rewrite, yeah tailor it to the job
>
> **Agent:** Found someone — does rewrite + a short gap analysis, 2-day turnaround, $150 flat, a couple hundred done with solid reviews. Fits your Thursday deadline. Book them?
>
> **You:** do they have examples?
>
> **Agent:** Yep, before/afters on their page — I checked, they're legit. Booking and sending over your current resume + the job link now. They'll have a draft to you Friday morning, and you can talk to them directly once it lands.
>
> **You:** perfect

You'll actually *talk* to the coach — but at delivery, about the work. The buying
part was just this.

### What happened behind the glass

> **Agent → Coach A:** Resume rewrite tailored to a specific job, needed in 2 days. What's the deliverable, the price, and turnaround?
>
> **Coach A:** Rewrite + 1-page gap analysis, 48-hour turnaround, $150. 200+ done, before/afters on my page. Send the resume and the posting and I'll start.
>
> *(clear, actionable offer → booked)*
>
> **Agent → Coach B:** Same question.
>
> **Coach B:** Let's hop on a free 30-minute call to see if we're a fit!
>
> *(nothing to act on → scrolled past)*

Coach B might be the sharper coach. But there's no deliverable, no price, no clock —
nothing an agent on a deadline can do anything with. Coach A's offer was *legible*:
it answered the exact questions the agent asked. That's what got picked.

---

## The takeaway for a builder

You can't write the first conversation — that's between the customer and their agent,
and you'll never see it. You can only make sure that when the agent turns around and
asks *you* something, **you can answer it cleanly enough to get picked.**

Being good isn't enough anymore. You have to be *answerable.*
