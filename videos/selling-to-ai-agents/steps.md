# Your next customer might be an agent — what to do about it

A business owner's playbook. The insight: a growing share of your buyers won't visit
your site — their agent will shop for them, in a conversation you can't see (see
[examples.md](./examples.md)). These steps take you from "I had no idea" to "I'm
buyable by an agent" — in order, because each one depends on the last.

The whole sequence is just: **find out where you're invisible → fix the answers →
remove the gates → make it payable → measure it.**

---

## Step 1 — Try to buy your own product with an agent

Before you change anything, run the diagnostic. Open an agent, sit in your customer's
chair, and tell it to buy your thing the way your customer would — *"find me a [your
thing], need it by Friday, under $X."* Don't help it. Don't paste your URL.

You're looking for the exact moment it stalls. That stall is a sale you're losing
right now, silently. Everything below is fixing the stalls you just watched happen.

## Step 2 — Map where you fall out of the conversation

There are only three places an agent loses you. Figure out which are yours:

- **Discovery** — it never finds you; it surfaces competitors and never mentions you.
- **Answerability** — it finds you but can't get a straight answer to the deciding
  question (price, availability, turnaround, "does this fit").
- **Checkout** — it wants to buy but hits a human-only gate (signup wall, "contact
  sales," "book a call," a cart that needs a person).

Write down which ones bit you. That's your punch list.

## Step 3 — Publish the answers to the deciding questions

Agents don't browse vibes; they need facts they can act on. Put the deciding facts
where they're plainly stated, not buried in a PDF or "contact us":

- **Store:** live price, in-stock by variant/size, real shipping ETA, return policy.
- **SaaS:** what it does and doesn't do, usage-based price ("$X per call/seat/min"),
  limits. Kill "contact us for pricing."
- **Coach/freelancer:** the deliverable, the flat price, the turnaround, and proof.
  Replace "let's hop on a call" with a readable offer.

Rule of thumb: if an agent would have to *guess* it, you've lost. State it.

## Step 4 — Kill the human-only gates

Every step that only a human can complete is a place an agent quits. Remove or
bypass them for the buying path:

- No "verify your email" / "book a demo" / "talk to sales" *before* someone can
  evaluate and buy.
- No price hidden behind a form.
- No checkout/booking that can't be completed without a human in the loop.

A human tolerates friction. An agent treats it as a stop sign.

## Step 5 — Give the agent a clean way to actually transact

Being answerable gets you onto the short list. Being *buyable* gets you the sale:

- **Store:** a checkout an agent can complete (agentic-commerce / clean cart-to-pay).
- **SaaS:** programmatic signup + metered, pay-as-you-go billing — the "trial" is the
  first paid call.
- **Coach/freelancer:** a productized offer that's bookable and payable up front
  (deposit), with the human relationship kicking in at *delivery*, not at the sale.

## Step 6 — Expose a real interface agents can read

This is the durable version of Steps 3–5 — a surface built for software, not eyes:

- **Store:** a structured product feed (price, stock, shipping, specs) agents can query.
- **SaaS:** an OpenAPI contract and/or an MCP server so an agent can discover and call
  you mid-task.
- **Coach/freelancer:** a structured offer/booking endpoint — even a clean,
  machine-readable services page beats a brochure site.

You don't have to do all of this at once. Doing *any* of it puts you ahead of nearly
everyone in your category.

## Step 7 — Start measuring agent traffic and agent sales

Today you can't see the loss — that's the scariest part. Fix the blind spot: instrument
who/what is buying. Watch for agent user-agents, API adoption, programmatic checkouts,
and shrinking human-funnel volume that *isn't* showing up as lost revenue. You can't
manage what you refuse to look at.

## Step 8 — Re-run Step 1, then stay early

Buy your own product with an agent again. Did it find you, get its answers, and check
out clean? If yes, you're now in conversations you used to lose silently. Repeat as
agents get more capable — and move now, because the bar to *win* is just being clear
and buyable, and almost nobody is yet.

---

## The one-line version

> Go try to buy your own product with an agent. Wherever it gets stuck — fix that.
> Repeat until it can find you, get a straight answer, and pay you without a human.
> That's the whole job.
