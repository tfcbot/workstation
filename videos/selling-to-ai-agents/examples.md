# What buying-with-an-agent feels like — from your customer's chair

Three stories. Each one shows the same thing twice: **what your customer
experiences** when they buy through an agent, and then **what that looks like from
your side of the counter** — because that second part is the one that should keep
you up at night.

The whole point: your customer's buying experience is moving into a room you're not
in. And when you lose, you lose *silently* — no bounce, no abandoned cart, no lead
that got away. You just quietly stop being in the running.

---

## 1. You run an online store

### Your customer's experience

Marcus is half-watching TV when his daughter mentions her cross-country meet is
Saturday and her trail shoes have a hole in them. He's not driving to a mall on a
Wednesday night. He picks up his phone and thinks out loud at it:

> *"Macy needs new trail shoes, the meet's Saturday. Kid's size 5ish. Nothing crazy
> expensive."*

Messy — no exact size, no real budget. His agent asks the one thing that matters
first: *"Saturday means it has to ship tomorrow — only show options that'll make
it?"* Yeah. It comes back with **three** — the way a friend who already did the
research would. One's cheap but "runs tiny." One's perfect but lands Monday. The
third is $74, arrives Thursday, true to size. Marcus says "that one." The only thing
his thumbs actually do is approve the payment. Total time: twenty seconds. **He never
saw a website.**

### From your side of the counter

Picture that whole thing from where *you* sit. Your homepage — never loaded. Your
hero video, your reviews, the 10%-off popup you A/B tested for a month — never seen.
The customer's experience of "shopping for shoes" happened entirely inside a chat you
have no window into.

Now the part that stings: if your store *made* that short list, you got the sale and
never lifted a finger. If it didn't — and a couple of stores that probably had the
right shoe *didn't* — there's no trace of it. No visit in your analytics. No cart to
recover. No "almost." Your dashboard looks like a normal Wednesday. You weren't
beaten on price or product. **You just weren't in the conversation, and nothing told
you.**

## 2. You sell a software tool / API

### Your customer's experience

Your customer is a builder, mid-project, in flow. Their coding agent hits a wall — it
needs transcription to finish a feature. The builder doesn't open ten pricing pages.
They just tell their agent to handle it and keep going.

The agent shops the way the builder *would* if they had the patience: it finds a
service where the whole deal is right there — endpoint, price, pennies, just call it —
runs a 30-second test, sees it works, and wires it in. Cost so far: forty cents. The
builder finds out at breakfast that it's already done. **They never evaluated a
vendor. They never saw a demo. They never talked to anyone.**

### From your side of the counter

This is your customer's purchase — and the entire thing you built your funnel around
just got skipped. The landing page, the feature comparison, the 14-day trial, the
"book a demo" CTA, the nurture sequence — all of it is choreography for a human who
never showed up. An agent walked in instead, and the moment it hit your signup wall or
your "contact sales for API access," it turned around.

You didn't get a trial signup that went cold. You didn't get a demo no-show. You got
**nothing** — because the agent's whole evaluation, the part where it decided you were
too much friction to bother with, took half a second and left no footprint. The
"better" product loses here all the time, and the founder never finds out why.

## 3. You're a coach, consultant, or freelancer

### Your customer's experience

Priya wants a job and the application closes Thursday. Her resume is three years
stale. She tells her agent to find someone good to fix it, fast.

The agent skims the way Priya would — looking for someone who just says what you get,
what it costs, and how long it takes. It finds an offer that answers all of it up
front: rewrite plus a gap analysis, two days, $150, "done a couple hundred of these."
That's a yes. It books, pays the deposit, sends over her resume and the job posting.
Priya said one sentence. **She'll talk to the person at delivery — but the *sale*
already closed without a single call.**

### From your side of the counter

Here's the one that hits coaches and consultants hardest: your *whole sales motion*
is usually the call. "Let's hop on a quick 30 to see if we're a fit" — that's where
you win people, with your warmth, your insight, your read of the room.

An agent never gets on that call. To an agent on a deadline, "let's hop on a call" is
a dead end — no deliverable, no price, no clock, nothing to act on — so it scrolls
right past you to the person whose offer it could actually *read.* The more talented
coach loses to the more legible one. And again: no inquiry, no "I'll think about it,"
no ghosted email. **Your calendar just stays a little emptier than it should, with no
reason attached.**

## The thread — why this should land

None of this is sci-fi. It's just *how people already shop* — react to a short list,
bail on anything that makes you work, pick the one that gives straight answers —
except now an agent does the legwork, in a conversation you can't see.

For you, the business owner, that means three things change at once:

1. **Your storefront isn't your storefront anymore.** The customer's experience is
   the chat with their agent. Your site is just one source the agent may or may not
   bother to read.
2. **Being the best stops being enough.** The agent picks the one it can get a
   straight answer out of and actually buy from — not the one with the nicest brand.
3. **Losing went invisible.** No bounce, no abandoned cart, no cold lead. You don't
   get told you lost. You just quietly stop showing up in your customers' results.

The good news is the flip side of #2 and #3: the bar to *win* is just being clear and
buyable — and almost nobody is, yet.

---

## Step 1 — go try to buy your own product with an agent

Don't take my word for any of this. Before you change a thing, do the one exercise
that makes it real: **open an agent and try to buy your own product through it.**

Sit in your customer's chair. Tell the agent what your customer would say — *"find
me a [your thing], I need it by Friday, under $X"* — and then just watch. Don't help
it. Don't paste your URL. Let it shop the way your customer's agent will.

Watch for the moment it gets stuck:

- Does it even *find* you, or does it surface three competitors and never mention you?
- When it asks the deciding question — *price, availability, turnaround, does this
  fit* — can it get a straight answer, or does it have to give up and guess?
- Can it actually *buy*, or does it hit a signup wall, a "contact sales," a "book a
  call," a cart that needs a human?

Wherever it stalls is exactly where your real customers' agents are stalling right
now — silently, with no trace in your analytics. That stall *is* the lost sale. You
just got to watch one happen for the first time.

Do this once and the rest of this isn't a theory anymore. It's a to-do list.
