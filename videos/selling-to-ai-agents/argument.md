# AI Native Sales — the argument

**Overarching theme:** *AI Native Sales* — ways to **sell** with AI.
**The argument:** escape the endless **AI productivity loop.**

> Built on *Simple Marketing for Smart People* (Tiago Forte & Billy Broas).
> **Marketing is the art of making an argument.** The One Question: *what does my
> viewer need to believe in order to act?* We don't push — we lay down premises
> they already accept, so the conclusion becomes inevitable. Each proof point
> lights up one of the **Five Lightbulbs**: Problem → Mechanism → Vehicle → Proof → Urgency.

---

## The enemy — the AI productivity loop

Everyone is using AI the same way: to do the work they already do, faster. Write
the email quicker, draft the doc quicker, ship more output. It feels like progress
but it's a treadmill — the gains get competed away because everyone has the same
tools, and productivity has **no finish line**. You optimize forever and the
revenue line doesn't move. That's the loop. This video is about getting *out* of it.

**The escape is the sell side.** Productivity is cost reduction; it shrinks. Sales
is where AI actually compounds — and there's a brand-new market on the sell side
that the productivity crowd can't see, because they're heads-down optimizing
tasks: **the buyers are becoming agents.** AI Native Sales = stop pointing AI at
your to-do list, start pointing it at the new buyer.

---

## The conclusion we want the viewer to reach

> **Stop grinding the productivity loop. Build a service an AI agent can discover,
> call, and pay for — and sell into the agent market while it's early.**

## The One Question

What must the viewer believe before that move is obvious?

1. The productivity loop is a **dead end** — optimizing tasks won't move revenue. *(Problem)*
2. Agents are **real buyers today**, not a 2027 someday. *(Problem)*
3. **Being early is the whole edge.** *(Urgency)*

Everything below earns those beliefs with premises the viewer can verify
independently — neutral source, dated milestone, on-the-record quote. No hype.

---

## The premises

### Premise 1 — The audience already flipped *(Problem)*

The web you're optimizing for humans is now the minority. In **April 2026**,
automated traffic crossed over human traffic for the first time — bots are now
**~56–57% of all web requests**. You're already building for a room that's more
than half machines.

### Premise 2 — These aren't crawlers. They're *agents* *(Mechanism)*

The fastest-growing slice isn't passive scraping — it's **agentic AI: bots
acting on a person's behalf.** That category grew **~8,000% in 2025**. A crawler
reads your page. An agent *does the task* — including the buying. The thing
showing up to your service has intent and a budget.

### Premise 3 — So a human storefront is the wrong storefront *(Vehicle)*

If your customer arrives as an agent, a landing page tuned for eyeballs is
pointing the wrong way. The right storefront is a **metered contract an agent can
call and pay per use** — discovery, signup, and settlement all machine-legible.
*(This is where Workstation enters — as the inevitable consequence of P1–P3, not
as a pitch.)*

### Premise 4 — The window is the proof *(Urgency)*

The person who watches ~20% of all internet traffic — Cloudflare's CEO — predicted
this crossover for **2027**. It happened ~18 months early. His words:
*"it happened faster than I predicted."* When the best-informed forecaster is
caught short, "I'll get to it next year" is a bet against the trend line.
