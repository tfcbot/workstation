# AI Native Sales — concepts

The vocabulary this video introduces and leans on. These are the named ideas a
viewer should walk away able to repeat. They thread through [argument.md](./argument.md),
[points.md](./points.md), and [ai-sales-loop.md](./ai-sales-loop.md).

---

## Core frame

### AI Native Sales
Using AI on the **sell side** — to win new buyers and create new markets — rather
than on the cost side to do existing work faster. The umbrella theme of the video.

### The AI Productivity Loop *(the trap)*
Pointing AI at your own to-do list: faster emails, more drafts, more output. Feels
like progress, but the gains get competed away (everyone has the same tools) and
revenue stays flat. Productivity has no finish line — you optimize forever. The
thing this video argues you must escape.

### The AI Sales Loop *(the escape)*
The compounding sell-side flywheel that replaces the treadmill:
**Package → Expose → Convert (via conversation) → Learn → Reinvest.** Each turn
makes the next sale easier. See [ai-sales-loop.md](./ai-sales-loop.md).

## The mechanism

### Agent-Led Sales
The buyer is an **agent**, not a person — it discovers, evaluates, negotiates, and
pays on a human's behalf. The agent *leads* the purchase: it asks the questions,
runs the comparison, and pulls the trigger. You're no longer selling to a human
who clicks; you're selling to software with intent and a budget.

### Conversation as Conversion
The conversion mechanism is **the conversation, not the click.** Agents don't scan
a page and hit "Buy" — they ask what you do, what it costs, whether you fit, then
call and pay. You convert by being *answerable*: your pricing, capabilities, and
terms have to survive being asked about, not just looked at.

### Agent-Buyable Service
A service exposed behind a **metered contract an agent can discover, call, and
settle per use** — signup, usage, and payment all machine-legible. The right
storefront when your customer arrives with an API key instead of a browser.

### Agent-Legibility
The new discoverability. Instead of ranking on Google for a human, you get found,
understood, and trusted by a model. The new "above the fold" is a clear contract
and a clean description an agent can parse.

### Agent Discovery Optimization (ADO)
The discipline that replaces SEO. SEO optimizes for a *human who searches, scans a
page, and clicks*; ADO optimizes for an *agent that queries, reads a contract, and
buys.* The job changes from "rank and get the click" to "get found, get a straight
answer, and get transacted with."

| | Classical SEO | Agent Discovery Optimization |
|---|---|---|
| Optimizes for | a human searcher | an agent acting for a human |
| Win condition | rank → get the click | get shortlisted → get bought |
| What you tune | keywords, backlinks, page speed, CTR | machine-readable facts (price, stock, terms), API/feed/MCP availability, answerability |
| Conversion surface | the landing page | the conversation + the checkout |
| Fail mode | page 2 of Google | not in the agent's short list — and you never know |

You don't "rank" for an agent. You either give it a straight, actionable answer and a
way to buy, or you're silently left out of the result it hands its human. *(Closely
related to [Agent-Legibility]; ADO is the active practice, legibility is the property
it's optimizing for.)*

## The proof spine

### The Crossover
April 2026: automated traffic passed human traffic for the first time (~56–57% of
web requests). The dated milestone that proves the audience already flipped.

### Earlier Than Predicted
Cloudflare's CEO had the crossover pegged for 2027; it landed ~18 months early
("it happened faster than I predicted"). The urgency proof — the trend is
accelerating, so being early is the whole edge.
